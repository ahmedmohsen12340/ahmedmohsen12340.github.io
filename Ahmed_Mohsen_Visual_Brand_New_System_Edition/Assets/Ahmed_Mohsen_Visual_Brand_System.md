# Ahmed Mohsen — Visual Brand System

**Version:** 1.0  
**Purpose:** A long-lived personal identity for digital and physical communication.  
**Applies to:** Websites, portfolios, presentations, documents, posters, social media, advertisements, thumbnails, business cards, and future creative work.

---

## 1. Brand foundation

### Brand idea

**Quiet precision. Grounded confidence.**

The identity should feel considered, disciplined, capable, and approachable. It should communicate quality through restraint rather than decoration. It must remain relevant as roles, industries, experience, and projects change.

### Personality

- **Professional:** composed, credible, and clear.
- **Disciplined:** consistent alignment, hierarchy, spacing, and color use.
- **Precise:** deliberate details, readable typography, and purposeful graphics.
- **Grounded:** warm neutral materials rather than cold, synthetic effects.
- **Distinctive:** deep evergreen paired with parchment and restrained burnt copper.

### What this identity is not

- Not tied to a particular job title, employer, technology, or career stage.
- Not a generic blue-purple technology brand.
- Not futuristic, neon, cyberpunk, or gaming-inspired.
- Not loud, overly playful, glossy, or decorative.

**The name is the brand anchor.** Use **Ahmed Mohsen** as the primary wordmark. A simple **AM** monogram may be used as a compact secondary mark, but it must remain typographic and understated.

---

## 2. Core visual direction

The visual language combines:

1. **Evergreen** for trust, stability, and recognition.
2. **Warm paper neutrals** for clarity, restraint, and a human feel.
3. **Burnt copper** for a small, memorable point of emphasis.
4. **A clear sans-serif type system** for strong communication.
5. **A disciplined grid** for consistency across every format.

The default expression is light and editorial. Dark evergreen layouts are an equally valid secondary expression for covers, hero sections, presentation openers, and high-impact campaign graphics.

### Visual keywords

`Grounded` · `Precise` · `Understated` · `Editorial` · `Timeless` · `Structured` · `Confident`

### 3.4 Color proportions

Use this as a starting balance, not a rigid mathematical rule:

- **65–75%** warm neutrals: Parchment, Ivory, Fog, or open space.
- **20–30%** Evergreen family and dark neutrals.
- **Up to 5%** Burnt Copper or Copper Ink.

For a dark composition, reverse the dominant background to Deep Pine or Dark Surface, keep text light, and retain copper as a small accent.

### 3.5 Color-use rules

1. Evergreen is the primary brand identifier. Use it consistently for the wordmark, important headings, and primary actions.
2. Parchment is the signature canvas. Prefer it to stark pure white for large backgrounds and print layouts.
3. Ink is the default text color on light backgrounds. Do not use Evergreen for every paragraph.
4. Copper is an accent, not a second primary color. Use it for a key number, short underline, small icon, divider, or one selected detail.
5. Use Copper Ink (`#9D563D`) for small copper text on light backgrounds. Use Copper Light (`#D18A68`) for copper text on dark backgrounds.
6. Mid Pine is supporting color. Do not allow it to compete with Evergreen.
7. Use Line for structure, not decoration. Borders should be quiet.
8. Do not introduce extra colors for individual projects or campaigns unless they have a clear functional meaning. The personal identity should remain recognizable.
9. Avoid pure black (`#000000`) and pure white (`#FFFFFF`) as large surfaces. Use the defined near-black and warm-white colors instead.
10. Never apply a gradient to the core brand colors. Prefer solid fills and deliberate contrast.

## 4. Typography system

Use only two type families in the core identity. This keeps the visual language recognizable and easy to reproduce.

### 4.1 Primary family — IBM Plex Sans

**Role:** All main communication: headlines, paragraphs, navigation, captions, labels, presentations, documents, and advertising copy.

**Preferred weights:**

- 400 Regular — paragraphs and long-form reading.
- 500 Medium — navigation, emphasis, short labels.
- 600 SemiBold — subheadings and section titles.
- 700 Bold — main headlines and strong calls to action.

**Character:** clear, measured, technical without feeling cold, and suitable for both digital and print communication.

### 4.2 Secondary family — IBM Plex Mono

**Role:** Use sparingly for functional or technical details, such as:

- Dates and timelines.
- Small category labels.
- Issue numbers and document references.
- Short metrics or data points.
- Code snippets and technical annotations.
- Small uppercase eyebrow labels.

**Preferred weights:** 400 Regular and 500 Medium.

Do not use the monospace family for long paragraphs, large blocks of marketing copy, or every heading. It is a supporting voice, not the main voice.

### 4.3 Font fallback

Use these fallbacks when the preferred font is unavailable:

- Sans: `Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif`
- Mono: `Consolas, Menlo, monospace`

Load the same font files and weights across channels wherever possible. Do not substitute a different decorative font for a one-off poster or campaign.

### 4.4 Typography hierarchy

The sizes below are reference values for screen design. Use responsive scaling and preserve the hierarchy rather than forcing identical sizes in every format.

| Style | Desktop reference | Weight | Line height | Typical use |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Display | 64 px | 700 | 1.05 | Main statement, cover, campaign headline |
| H1 | 48 px | 700 | 1.10 | Page title |
| H2 | 36 px | 600 | 1.15 | Main section heading |
| H3 | 28 px | 600 | 1.20 | Subsection heading |
| H4 | 22 px | 600 | 1.25 | Card or content heading |
| Lead | 20 px | 400 | 1.50 | Introductory paragraph |
| Body | 16–18 px | 400 | 1.55–1.65 | Main reading text |
| Small | 14 px | 400 | 1.45 | Supporting text |
| Eyebrow | 12–13 px | 500 | 1.30 | Short category labels; Mono optional |

On mobile, reduce Display to approximately 40–44 px and H1 to approximately 34–38 px. Keep body text comfortably readable; do not shrink it simply to fit more content.

### 4.5 Typography rules

1. Use sentence case for most headings and labels.
2. Reserve uppercase for short labels, never long headings or paragraphs.
3. Use bold weight to establish hierarchy; do not rely on underlines, outlines, or shadows.
4. Keep paragraphs left-aligned in most cases. Center-align only short statements or compact compositions.
5. Keep line lengths moderate. Avoid full-width paragraphs on desktop.
6. Use generous line spacing for body copy and tighter line spacing for large headlines.
7. Avoid excessive letter spacing. Small mono labels may use slight positive tracking; large headlines may use slightly tight tracking.
8. Do not use more than two type families in one composition.
9. Do not stretch, skew, outline, or apply decorative effects to the type.
10. Preserve clear hierarchy when adapting the same content to different formats.

## 5. Layout and composition language

The same layout principles should work for a website, a poster, a presentation, or a social post.

### 5.1 Grid

- Build layouts on a consistent grid.
- For websites and presentations, use a 12-column grid on wide screens and simplify to 4 columns on mobile.
- Align headings, text blocks, images, and repeated elements to shared edges.
- Use an 8-point spacing rhythm: 8, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64, 80, and 96 px. A 4 px adjustment is acceptable for optical alignment.
- Keep margins generous. Empty space is part of the identity, not wasted space.

### 5.2 Composition

- Prefer clear hierarchy over symmetry for its own sake.
- Use strong alignment and intentional asymmetry when it improves the composition.
- Give each design one primary focal point.
- Use short supporting details rather than filling every available area.
- Use thin rules, restrained framing, and simple geometric blocks to organize information.
- Keep visual density low to medium. Do not make every item compete for attention.

### 5.3 Shape language

- Favor rectangles, clean edges, simple dividers, and restrained corner radii.
- Use small-to-medium corner radii for digital containers; avoid excessively rounded, pill-heavy layouts.
- Use circles only when they have a clear purpose, such as a profile image, avatar, or simple marker.
- Do not use random blobs, floating orbs, excessive glass effects, or decorative abstract shapes.

## 6. Image and photography direction

Images should reinforce credibility and feel authentic. They should not look like generic technology stock imagery.

### Preferred direction

- Natural, believable photography with controlled lighting.
- Clean architecture, structured workspaces, materials, city details, and real working environments where relevant.
- Professional portraits that look relaxed, confident, and genuine.
- Neutral or slightly warm color grading that sits naturally beside Parchment and Evergreen.
- Clear subject framing, calm backgrounds, and enough negative space for text when needed.
- Real project imagery or product screenshots when they genuinely help explain the work.

### Avoid

- AI-generated robot heads, glowing brains, artificial neural networks, or generic futuristic dashboards.
- Blue-purple gradients, neon circuits, holographic effects, and cyberpunk imagery.
- Obvious stock-photo poses or exaggerated corporate handshakes.
- Heavy filters, artificial skin retouching, oversaturated colors, or excessive blur.
- Images with so much detail that the text becomes difficult to read.

### Image treatment

- Keep colors natural and restrained.
- Use a consistent crop style within the same asset or campaign.
- For text over imagery, use a solid or sufficiently opaque Evergreen/Deep Pine overlay only when necessary for contrast.
- Do not place small text directly over a busy photograph.
- Use borders or simple frames rather than ornamental shadows.

## 7. Graphic elements and iconography

### Graphic elements

- Use thin lines, dividers, simple grids, numbered markers, and restrained geometric framing.
- A small copper rule or marker can create a recognizable brand signature.
- Use diagrams only when they clarify an idea.
- Keep charts and diagrams clean, labeled, and easy to understand without relying on color alone.

### Icons

- Prefer simple outline icons with consistent stroke weight.
- Use one icon family within a design.
- Use Evergreen or Ink for standard icons; use Copper only for a deliberate highlight.
- Avoid colorful 3D icons, emoji as interface icons, and mixed icon styles.

### Data and charts

- Primary series: Evergreen.
- Secondary series: Mid Pine or neutral gray.
- Highlight or threshold: Burnt Copper.
- Background and grid: Parchment, Fog, and Line.
- Always include labels, legends, or direct annotations. Do not depend on color alone to distinguish information.

## 8. Application across formats

### 8.1 Websites and digital products

- Use Parchment or Ivory as the default page canvas.
- Use Evergreen for primary actions, key navigation states, and important section headings.
- Use Ink for long-form text and Muted Text for secondary information.
- Use Deep Pine or Dark Surface for selected high-impact sections, not necessarily every page.
- Keep Copper limited to a small number of meaningful highlights.
- Maintain generous spacing and consistent alignment.
- Ensure the identity remains recognizable without gradients, animation, or decorative effects.

### 8.2 Posters and presentations

- Start with one strong headline and one clear supporting message.
- Use Parchment for an editorial, light composition or Deep Pine for a formal, high-impact cover.
- Keep the wordmark in a consistent position within a series.
- Use a small copper marker, line, or number as a recurring detail.
- Use large type, strong margins, and a simple hierarchy that can be understood at a glance.
- On presentation slides, use one main idea per slide and keep recurring title, footer, and page-number placements consistent.

### 8.3 Social media and advertising

- Make the main message readable at thumbnail size.
- Use one focal point, one headline, and one clear supporting element.
- Keep the brand name or monogram in a consistent, unobtrusive location.
- Use the same palette and typography across square, portrait, and landscape versions.
- Do not add extra colors just to make an advertisement more attention-grabbing.
- Avoid dense text, multiple competing calls to action, and template-like decorative effects.

### 8.4 Documents and professional materials

- Use Parchment or Ivory backgrounds, Ink text, Evergreen headings, and Line dividers.
- Use IBM Plex Sans for reading and IBM Plex Mono for dates, references, or technical labels.
- Keep title pages and section headings visually related to the wider identity.
- Prioritize legibility, clear hierarchy, and print-friendly contrast.

### 8.5 Print production

- HEX and RGB values are the source of truth for digital use.
- For print, convert colors using the printer's specified CMYK profile and paper stock. Do not assume one universal CMYK conversion will look correct on every printer.
- Request a physical or calibrated proof for important print runs, especially for Evergreen and Copper.
- Check that thin lines, small text, and muted colors remain readable after printing.
- Use uncoated or textured paper only when it supports the intended calm, editorial feel and does not compromise legibility.

## 9. Brand consistency rules

### Always

- Use the exact palette values in this document.
- Use IBM Plex Sans and IBM Plex Mono as the only core type families.
- Keep Evergreen as the primary identifying color.
- Use warm neutrals as the visual foundation.
- Use Copper sparingly and intentionally.
- Keep alignment, spacing, and hierarchy disciplined.
- Choose authentic, restrained imagery.
- Design for legibility before decoration.

### Never

- Add generic blue-to-purple or cyan-to-magenta gradients.
- Use neon glows, glassmorphism, holographic surfaces, or cyberpunk styling.
- Introduce a new accent color for every project.
- Use multiple unrelated fonts in one design.
- Apply heavy shadows, excessive rounded cards, or ornamental effects everywhere.
- Use tiny low-contrast text over images.
- Distort the wordmark or monogram.
- Make the identity depend on a current role, technology stack, employer, or project.

## 11. Guidance for future design generation

When using this document as a prompt or reference for a design tool, follow these instructions:

1. Treat this file as the source of truth for color, typography, and visual direction.
2. Do not invent a new palette or add trendy gradient colors.
3. Use the exact HEX values; do not approximate the brand colors.
4. Use IBM Plex Sans for the main typographic system and IBM Plex Mono only for secondary technical details.
5. Keep the composition clean, structured, warm, and confident.
6. Apply the same identity whether the requested output is a website, poster, presentation, social post, advertisement, document, or another format.
7. Adapt the layout to the medium, but do not change the brand's visual character.
8. Use placeholder content only where necessary; do not invent personal achievements, credentials, companies, or facts.

**The identity should be recognizable even when the name is removed: through its evergreen-and-parchment palette, restrained copper accent, typography, and disciplined composition.**
